HOW TO PLAY

Look ahead. Make room.

  1. Choose food each week. Cooking costs $35 and one time block. Convenience costs $70 and no blocks. The community food option costs no cash, one block and 10 energy.
  2. Read the calendar. Cash now is not automatically safe to spend. Paydays arrive before that day’s choices; bills settle automatically.
  3. Use time deliberately. Each week has three blocks. Optional shifts, rest, friends and goals use them. Unused blocks expire at week’s end. Baseline work uses 10 energy at each week’s start; the opening 65 energy already includes that first reduction.
  4. Preview, then confirm. Money, time and energy costs appear on each choice. Nothing moves while you read. One card may skip several fictional days.
  5. Follow consequences. Bike inspection reveals a minor or major fault; waiting has a disclosed later cost. Rest raises energy by 20. Extra shifts need 30 energy and are limited to one per week.
Bills, setbacks and forecasts

If you cannot afford a bill, it remains fully outstanding with a single $15 late fee. No daily compounding. Income settles overdue bills oldest first when affordable. Optional spending and commitments wait until debts settle; food, rest, extra shifts and essential bike repair remain available.

Forecast subtracts known bills and outstanding balances before the next paycheck. It excludes future food and decisions. It is a partial forecast, not a spending recommendation.

From day 14, an unrepaired bike blocks extra shifts. Base pay still arrives. You can repair from Make room. An inspection keeps its quoted repair cost until day 14; waiting then uses the higher disclosed cost.

Your result shows bills, cash, energy, your chosen goal and moments separately. Replay the same month to try another approach, or a different bike condition. All amounts and creditor rules are fictional scenario rules.
